**Modelling question:** How much dissolved gold can GolB-displaying cells
capture?

## Continuing from the leaching stage

Biorecovery continues from the gold dissolved during leaching. The remaining
printed-circuit-board solids are filtered from the vessel, leaving a liquid
that contains dissolved gold, copper and residual sulfur species.

GolB-displaying *Escherichia coli* are grown on a reusable, branched comb. The
comb has flat horizontal leaves that provide surfaces for the cells. It is
lowered into the filtered leachate for capture, removed from the vessel, washed
and then transferred to the gold-release stage.

## How we modelled biorecovery

### The chemical exchange represented by the model

Gold enters biorecovery as a soluble gold-thiosulfate complex. GolB presents a
Cys-X-X-Cys metal-binding motif, represented here as GolB-(SH)₂. Capture is
treated as ligand exchange between thiosulfate and this binding motif:

Au(S₂O₃)₂³⁻ + GolB-(SH)₂ ⇌ GolB-Au + 2S₂O₃²⁻

This is the capture event represented by the model. Gold leaves its soluble
thiosulfate complex and becomes associated with the cysteine binding site on
GolB. The equation groups the intermediate molecular steps into one reversible
exchange.

Copper is carried into the same stage. Its competing interaction is represented
schematically as:

Cu-thiosulfate + GolB-(SH)₂ ⇌ GolB-Cu + released thiosulfate

Copper is a competitor because GolB does not provide separate sites for gold
and copper. Au(I) and Cu(I) can both coordinate to the same two sulfur atoms in
the Cys-X-X-Cys binding motif. A GolB site occupied by Cu(I) is therefore not
available to capture Au(I). This matters even when GolB binds gold more
strongly, because the filtered leachate contains much more dissolved copper
than gold.

The competition model follows this reversible sharing of the GolB sites.
Cu(II) is kept separate because it can do something different: it may oxidise
the cysteine thiols and deactivate a site instead of temporarily occupying it.

### Symbols used in the main model

The model uses concentrations wherever a quantity is expressed relative to the
capture-liquid volume.

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---:|
| C_Au,T | Total dissolved gold entering capture | µM |
| C_Cu,T | Total dissolved copper entering capture | µM |
| C_Au | Free dissolved gold remaining at equilibrium | µM |
| C_Cu | Free dissolved copper remaining at equilibrium | µM |
| B_Au | Gold bound to the comb per liquid volume | µM |
| B_Cu | Copper bound to the comb per liquid volume | µM |
| S_T | Effective binding inventory per liquid volume | µM |
| K_Au | Apparent gold dissociation concentration | µM |
| K_Cu | Apparent copper dissociation concentration | µM |
| V_cap | Volume of filtered leachate contacted by the comb | L |

A smaller dissociation concentration represents stronger apparent binding.

## The main gold-capture model

We first ask how much gold can bind when a known GolB-bearing inventory contacts
the filtered leachate.

The effective binding inventory per liquid volume is:

S_T = (q_Au,ref × m_X) / V_cap

where:

- q_Au,ref is the reference gold loading per gram of dry cells.
- m_X is the dry cell biomass attached to the comb.
- V_cap is the volume contacted by the comb.

Using the value reported in the literature:

q_Au,ref = 4.62 µmol Au/g dry cells

Wei et al. measured this loading using surface-displayed GolB on *E. coli*
[S76]. The experiment used one overnight exposure to 50 µM Au(III) in LB, so
the value sets the current literature scenario; it is not a measured maximum
for this project's thiosulfate leachate.

The gold-only equations have not changed. The first equation says that gold
bound to the cells is no longer free in the liquid:

C_Au = C_Au,T − B_Au

The second is the original Langmuir equation. It relates the free gold to the
amount bound by the available GolB inventory:

B_Au = (S_T × C_Au) / (K_Au + C_Au)

Together, the equations divide the incoming gold between two places: gold that
remains dissolved and gold that leaves with the comb. The binding equation uses
the free concentration, C_Au, because some of the original feed has already
moved onto the cells.

The amount of gold captured is:

n_Au,captured = V_cap × B_Au

The fraction of incoming gold captured is:

η_Au,capture = B_Au / C_Au,T

This is the original gold-only result. We retain it as the reference for
showing what changes when copper is present.

## Why copper is included

The filtered leachate does not contain gold alone. In the current 72-hour
leaching scenario, it contains approximately 29 µM dissolved gold and 818 µM
total dissolved copper. The feed concentration ratio is therefore:

C_Cu,T / C_Au,T = 818 / 29 = 28.2

This value describes what enters the vessel. It does not tell us how the GolB
sites will be divided, because GolB does not bind the two metals equally.

We therefore added copper to the original gold-binding model. The purpose of
this addition is simple: determine how much of the GolB inventory still carries
gold when both metals are present.

## Modelling competition between gold and copper

Gold and copper share one competitive Langmuir denominator:

D = 1 + (C_Au / K_Au) + (C_Cu / K_Cu)

The bound-gold concentration is:

B_Au = S_T × (C_Au / K_Au) / D

The bound-copper concentration is:

B_Cu = S_T × (C_Cu / K_Cu) / D

Both metals are conserved:

C_Au = C_Au,T − B_Au

C_Cu = C_Cu,T − B_Cu

This keeps the structure of the original model. Each metal has a conservation
equation, while both metals share one binding expression. Sharing D means that
increasing copper occupancy reduces the inventory available to gold. The first
term in D allows some sites to remain empty.

## Representing selectivity

To compare gold and copper binding with one quantity, we use:

α = K_Cu / K_Au

Because K_Au and K_Cu are dissociation concentrations, a larger α means that
copper binds more weakly than gold. For each value of α:

K_Cu = α × K_Au

The model compares several values of α and shows how gold capture changes as
GolB becomes more selective for gold. This gives the wet-lab team a target:
measure whether the real cells provide enough selectivity at the copper-to-gold
ratio entering capture.

Purified GolB has published dissociation constants of 3.02 × 10⁻³⁷ M for Au(I)
and 1.61 × 10⁻¹⁷ M for Cu(I), giving an intrinsic ratio of approximately
5.3 × 10¹⁹ [S76]. The gold value was obtained by cyanide competition and the
copper value by a Cu(I)-BCS competitive-ligand method described in the
supplement. These measurements establish strong molecular preference for gold,
but they do not transfer directly to surface-displayed GolB in alkaline
thiosulfate leachate. They are therefore mechanistic evidence, not process
parameters.

Wei et al. also tested 1, 5 and 50 µM mixtures of several metal ions in Figures
5b and 5c. Those experiments reported metal recovery after papain treatment,
not a gold-only equilibrium isotherm, and cannot supply K_Au or K_Cu. The
reported value near 89% is the fraction of previously adsorbed gold released by
papain, not the fraction of all recovered metals that was gold.

## What the model currently shows

The current calculation uses:

- C_Au,T = 29 µM dissolved gold.
- C_Cu,T = 818 µM total dissolved copper.
- S_T = 23.1 µM effective binding inventory.
- q_Au,ref = 4.62 µmol Au/g dry cells.
- K_Au = 0.1 µM as the current recovery-model proxy.
- α as a selectivity sweep.

Because K_Au has not been measured in the project liquid, we also repeat the
calculation across the declared sensitivity cases. The table reports the
fraction of incoming gold captured:

| K_Au | α = 10 | α = 100 | α = 1,000 | α = 10,000 | Copper-free |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0001 µM | 18.21% | 50.81% | 72.31% | 78.62% | 79.65% |
| 0.1 µM, current proxy | 18.20% | 50.65% | 71.67% | 77.50% | 78.40% |
| 1 µM | 18.06% | 49.23% | 66.96% | 70.67% | 71.15% |
| 10 µM, Part II placeholder | 16.81% | 39.26% | 46.82% | 47.81% | 47.92% |

Across K_Au values from 0.0001 to 1 µM, the low-selectivity results change
little. The 10 µM placeholder produces a materially lower capture ceiling.
This shows why the copper-free value of 78.40% must also be described as
conditional on the current K_Au proxy.

For the current 0.1 µM proxy, gold and copper occupy equal shares at an α of
approximately 46.1, not at the feed ratio of 28.2. At α = 28.2, the model gives
approximately 41% of the inventory to gold and 59% to copper.

The practical result is that copper competition can affect recovery even when
GolB favours gold. Under the current proxy conditions, an apparent α near
1,000 is required to keep the loss of gold capture below approximately 10% of
the copper-free result.

## Model assumptions

1. Gold and copper compete for one effective binding inventory.
2. Each occupied unit of that inventory carries one metal ion.
3. The capture contact is long enough to approach equilibrium.
4. Mixing is sufficient for the liquid to be represented by uniform
   concentrations.
5. The 4.62 µmol Au/g dry cells result is transferred as a literature reference
   loading, not a project measurement or fitted maximum.
6. K_Au is an apparent process constant. Its current value is a proxy and its
   effect is shown through the sensitivity table.
7. K_Cu is represented through the α sweep until competitive measurements are
   available.
8. Cu(I) binding competition and Cu(II)-driven cysteine damage are separate
   processes.
9. Washing and elution occur after the equilibrium capture calculation.
10. Background adsorption to cells and hardware is not included in the
    reference calculation and must be separated experimentally.

<details>
<summary><strong>How comb area supplies the biomass input</strong></summary>

The hardware model converts the wetted comb area into attached dry biomass:

m_X = a_comb × σ_X

where:

- a_comb is the wetted, colonisable area on both sides of every leaf, in m².
- σ_X is the attached dry biomass per unit area, in g dry cells/m².

Until the real comb is measured, we use a provisional monolayer estimate. One
cell is assumed to occupy 2.0 µm², cells are assumed to cover the complete
surface, and the repository uses 2.36 × 10¹² cells/g dry biomass [S30].

σ_X = [1 / (2.0 × 10⁻¹² m²/cell)] / (2.36 × 10¹² cells/g)

σ_X = 0.212 g dry cells/m²

The primary literature-loading scenario therefore gives:

n_cap = q_Au,ref × σ_X × a_comb

n_cap = 4.62 × 0.212 × a_comb

n_cap = 0.979 × a_comb µmol Au

Each square metre provides a conditional gold-only inventory of 0.979 µmol.
Reproducing the previous 23.1 µmol inventory would require:

a_comb = 23.1 / (4.62 × 0.212)

a_comb = 23.6 m²

This translates the former 5 g biomass scenario into an area. It is not yet the
final comb specification. The required area must be recalculated after the
attached biomass and competitive binding parameters are measured.

</details>

## Measurements required to replace the proxy values

### 1. Measure the equilibrium contact time

Expose equivalent GolB-coated comb pieces to the same synthetic process liquid
and measure dissolved gold over time. Use a contact time after the remaining
gold concentration has stopped changing. This prevents slow uptake from being
mistaken for weak equilibrium binding.

### 2. Fit the gold-only isotherm

Use a synthetic liquid matching the intended pH, thiosulfate, glycine, ionic
strength and temperature. Measure the matrix-specific ICP-MS or ICP-OES
detection and quantification limits before selecting the lowest gold
concentrations.

The concentration series must include points below 1 µM. If the current
0.1 µM proxy is near the eventual result, measurements at only 1–100 µM would
all lie close to the loading plateau and would not identify K_Au.

A proposed initial series, subject to the measured quantification limit, is:

0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30 and 100 µM gold

Run a pilot first and adjust m_X/V_cap so the change between initial and
equilibrium gold remains large relative to analytical uncertainty without
exhausting almost all gold from the liquid.

Calculate the equilibrium loading from:

q_Au = [(C_Au,0 − C_Au,e) × V_cap] / m_X

Fit:

q_Au = q_Au,max × C_Au,e / (K_Au + C_Au,e)

This supplies the project-specific q_Au,max and K_Au. The fit should report
uncertainty, residuals and the concentration range used. If the experiment
does not reach a plateau, q_Au,max should not be reported as an identified
maximum.

### 3. Fit copper competition

After fitting the gold-only isotherm, hold gold near the expected process
concentration and vary copper from no copper to the concentration measured in
the filtered leachate. Measure both metals at equilibrium and fit all samples
together using the two-metal model.

The feed ratio of 28.2 provides a useful concentration scale for designing the
experiment, but it is not the equilibrium tie value. The design should include
conditions on both sides of the modelled transition.

If copper produces a measurable effect, fit K_Cu and calculate
α = K_Cu/K_Au. If no effect is resolved within the tested concentration and
analytical range, report a lower bound, α > x, rather than an exact estimate.

Include:

- GolB-displaying cells.
- Uninduced or non-GolB cells.
- A cell-free comb.
- A liquid-only stability blank.
- Independent biological replicates.
- Directly measured attached dry biomass.

Test Cu(I)-thiosulfate and Cu(II) separately where possible. The first tests
competition; the second can also reveal oxidative damage to GolB cysteines.

### 4. Follow both metals through the filter sequence

Measure gold and copper in:

1. The filtered leachate before capture.
2. The liquid remaining after capture.
3. The combined wash liquid.
4. The final eluate.

These measurements distinguish equilibrium capture from usable recovery. A
comb that binds gold but loses it during washing, or carries copper into the
eluate, has not yet achieved selective gold recovery.

## Conclusion

The biorecovery model begins with the amount of GolB-displaying biomass on the
comb and calculates how gold and copper divide its effective binding inventory.
It conserves both metals and connects the captured-gold amount directly to the
next process stage.

The current sensitivity analysis shows that copper competition can reduce gold
capture even when GolB favours gold, because copper is much more abundant in
the filtered leachate. The conclusion remains stable across the lower K_Au
cases, while the 10 µM placeholder produces a lower capture ceiling.

The next modelling improvement is therefore experimental rather than
structural: measure attached biomass, fit a gold-only isotherm in the intended
process liquid, and then fit the competitive response to copper. These results
will replace the current proxy and selectivity sweep without changing the main
model.
